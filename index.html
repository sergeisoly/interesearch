Some Studies of Language Model Weight Edits

Two experiments on how language-model weight edits affect related languages and whether a learned repair can be reused.

Some Studies of Language Model Weight Edits

I tested how weight edits affect predictions across languages and whether a correction learned for one fine-tune can help another.

First published 14 October 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026

Selective language editing

Worsening a model’s German predictions also affected its Dutch. How much depended on the edit. Across three language pairs, changing one weight subcomponent did the least damage to the related language when the edits were calibrated to the same target loss increase on validation text. LoRA and full-matrix fine-tuning preserved English predictions better.

Repair transfer

An earlier repair helped restore Dutch predictions on two new fine-tunes from the same model and recipe, while preserving the intended change to German. Training the repair vectors recovered more than rescaling them along their original directions. With little data and few steps, the earlier repair gave training a head start. At the largest budget, training from scratch nearly caught up.

About these studies

I learn best by trying a method myself. These studies grew out of a wish to understand earlier work on interpretability and model editing, then see what else those methods could reveal.

The studies take the form of research papers, though I still hesitate to call them rigorous scientific work. My hope is that someone else who wants to try these methods will find the experiments useful.
